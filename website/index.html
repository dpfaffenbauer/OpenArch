Arc closes.
OpenArch opens.

The sidebar browser you know, rebuilt as open source on real Chrome. Your spaces, logins and extensions move over in one step.

Download for Mac iPhone & iPad

Free and MIT licensed. macOS 15 or later on Apple silicon, signed and notarized. The source is on GitHub.

Everything comes along.

OpenArch reads your Arc data right on your Mac. No account, no upload, and Arc stays as it was.

  • Spaces with their themes
  • Favorites
  • Pinned tabs & folders
  • Open tabs & split views
  • Logins & cookies
  • Saved passwords
  • History
  • Extensions & their settings

Coming from somewhere else?

Bookmarks and history come over from every browser on your Mac. Each browser profile becomes a space of its own.

  • Chrome
  • Edge
  • Brave
  • Vivaldi
  • Opera
  • Dia
  • Helium
  • Comet
  • Firefox
  • Zen
  • Safari

From Chrome and the browsers built on it, your logins and saved passwords come too. Passwords from Firefox, Safari, 1Password or Bitwarden import from their CSV export.

The other browsers stay as they were. How the move from another browser works

Set up before the first tab opens.

The first time OpenArch opens, a small window walks you through it. A couple of minutes, and every step can be skipped.

  1. Bring your Arc

    Your spaces, favorites and pinned tabs build up in front of you, without a restart.

  2. One question from macOS

    It asks once for Arc’s key in your keychain, so your logins come along.

  3. Spaces and accounts

    See which Google account each profile is signed in to, and sign in where one is missing.

  4. Make it yours

    A color for each space, and light or dark for the pages.

  5. Default browser

    One click, and links from Mail and Slack open here.

The sidebar you already know.

No tab strip and no toolbar. Your tabs are on the left, and the page gets the rest of the window.

  • Favorites at the top, for the sites you use all the time.
  • Pinned tabs and folders, which belong to the space and stay.
  • Under the line, today’s tabs. They clear themselves away unless you pin them.

⌘S hides the sidebar, ⌘D pins a tab.

⌘T finds everything.

One field for addresses, searches, open tabs, pinned pages, history and commands. Enter takes you there, or straight back to the tab you already have.

  • Type yt or gh, press Tab, and search that site.
  • A whole question goes to Claude or ChatGPT.
  • new doc, new sheet or new ticket starts a fresh one.

Two pages, one view.

Drag a tab onto the page, or onto another tab in the sidebar. Up to four pages side by side or stacked, with dividers you can drag.

⌃⇧= splits the view, ⌃⇧S swaps the sides.

A space for every part of your day.

Each space has its own theme, pinned folders and tabs. Give it a profile and it keeps its own logins and extensions, so work and private never mix.

The theme picker is Arc’s: one to three colors that blend across the window, with intensity and grain. Themes from Arc come over exactly.

⌃1 to ⌃9 change spaces, and so does a sideways swipe over the sidebar. ⌃1 to ⌃4 work on this page too.

This page is a space too. Drag a handle.

Real Chrome underneath.

The sidebar is a native Mac app. The pages are Chrome: every tab is a real Chrome tab, on Chrome’s own engine, kept on its newest stable release.

  • Every extension from the Chrome Web Store, with its popups and side panels
  • DevTools, docked beside the page or in a window of their own
  • Chrome’s password manager, encrypted with a key in your login keychain
  • H.264, AAC and HEVC video
  • Chromium’s sandbox around every page

Extensions and Chrome features

Netflix and Spotify play.

Streaming services only certify their DRM for Chrome itself. So OpenArch opens them with Safari’s engine, in the same tab, and everything else with Chrome.

And all the little things.

In the sidebar

Peek
Links from pinned tabs open in a card over the page, and so does any link you ⇧-click. Keep it, split it, or Esc.
Tab previews
Rest the pointer on a tab and see its page, without leaving the one you are on.
Folder previews
Rest on a closed folder for a list of its tabs. Type to search it.
Unread dots
Gmail, Slack, WhatsApp and others get a dot on their icon while something is unread.
Select several
⇧-click a range of tabs, then move them, archive them or put them in a folder together.
GitHub live folders
Pinned folders that fill themselves with your pull requests, reviews or issues, with their checks.
Hover sidebar
Hide it, touch the window’s edge, and it floats back. It does that here too.

Calls and media

Meetings
Your next meeting at the bottom of the sidebar, a nudge before it starts, and a button that joins the call.
Calls keep going
Leave a call’s tab and it floats in a small window, with buttons to mute, turn off the camera and hang up.
Screen sharing
While a call shares a tab or your screen, the sidebar says what, and ends it with one click.
Now playing
What plays in another tab, or in Spotify, sits in the sidebar with its cover. Rest on it to scrub, skip or pause.
Picture in picture
Leave a tab with a video playing and it keeps going in a small window.

Tabs that look after themselves

Today’s tabs clear away
Tabs you did not pin go to the archive after twelve hours. Pinned ones stay.
Sleeping tabs
Tabs you have not looked at in a while free their memory and wake up when you come back.
Chat and mail stay awake
Pinned Gmail, Slack or WhatsApp never sleep, so messages and calls keep coming.
Short tab titles
Long, noisy titles get short names, made on your Mac by Apple Intelligence.
Tab switcher
Hold ⌃ and press Tab for the tabs you used last, each with a picture of its page.
Library
Downloads fly into the library button. Media, downloads, spaces and archived tabs in one searchable place.

HTTPS only

Claude next to your tabs.

Press ⌘E and a chat opens beside the page. Ask about what you are reading, add other tabs with @, or hand it the work: it reads the page, looks at it, scrolls, clicks and types in your real tabs, signed in as you.

Before it clicks or types the first time, it asks.

All of it runs Claude Code on your Mac, on your own login. No API key, and nothing in between.

Questions

Is this made by The Browser Company?

No. OpenArch is an independent open-source project. It is not affiliated with The Browser Company or Atlassian, and it contains no code from Arc.

What happens to my Arc data?

OpenArch only reads it. For logins and passwords, macOS asks you once whether OpenArch may use Arc’s key from your keychain. Arc keeps working as it was.

Will macOS let me open it?

Yes. Every build is signed with OpenArch’s Developer ID and notarized by Apple. macOS asks once whether to open an app downloaded from the internet.

What does OpenArch collect?

Nothing. There is no account, no analytics and no telemetry. After a crash it asks whether to send a report, and shows it to you first. Everything OpenArch itself sends anywhere is listed under Data and privacy.

Do Netflix and Spotify work?

Yes, with Safari’s engine, in the same tab. Chrome extensions do not run on those pages, and you sign in to each service once more.

How does OpenArch update?

By itself, on a Stable or Nightly channel. It checks GitHub at launch and every six hours, only installs builds signed with OpenArch’s Developer ID, and never goes back to an older one.

Do I need Claude?

No. The AI features need the Claude Code command line tool on your Mac, signed in. Without it they say so, and the rest of OpenArch works as usual.

What is not there yet?

Arc’s Easels and Notes have no counterpart yet, Google does not offer Chrome sync to other browsers, and there is no build for Intel Macs. The whole list is under Known limitations.
