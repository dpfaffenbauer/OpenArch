Arc closes.
OpenArch opens.

An open-source browser for Mac with the sidebar, Spaces and command bar you are used to. It runs on real Chrome, so every extension keeps working, and it takes your spaces, logins and extensions over from Arc in one step.

Download for Mac Source on GitHub

Free and MIT licensed. Early build for macOS 15 or later on Apple silicon.

Everything comes along.

Arc stops working on December 22, 2026. OpenArch reads your Arc data right on your Mac and takes it over. There is no account and nothing goes through a cloud.

  • Spaces with their colors and icons
  • Favorites
  • Pinned tabs, folders and subfolders
  • Open tabs and split views
  • Logins and cookies
  • Saved passwords
  • History
  • Extensions with their settings

Arc itself stays untouched, and whatever OpenArch had before is kept as a backup.

Real Chrome tabs. All your extensions.

Every tab in OpenArch is a full Chrome tab. Password managers fill in, ad blockers block, side panels open and DevTools dock, the way they do in Chrome. You install extensions from the Chrome Web Store as usual.

1Password, uBlock Origin Lite, Tampermonkey, React Developer Tools, MetaMask, Wappalyzer

New Chromium releases arrive on their own: a daily job builds OpenArch with each new stable version.

Let your agent drive.

Turn on AI control in the settings and OpenArch speaks Chrome’s DevTools protocol on your Mac. Claude Code, Playwright or Puppeteer can then open pages, click, fill in forms, read the console and take screenshots, in your real browser and with your logins. One line connects Claude Code:

claude mcp add openarch -- npx -y chrome-devtools-mcp@latest --browser-url=http://127.0.0.1:9222

Off by default. While it is on, an antenna in the sidebar shows it, because any program on your Mac could then steer the browser.

Questions

Is this made by The Browser Company?

No. OpenArch is an independent open-source project. It is not affiliated with The Browser Company or Atlassian, and it contains no code from Arc.

macOS says it can’t check OpenArch for malware.

Early builds are not notarized yet. Unzip OpenArch, move it to Applications, then run xattr -dr com.apple.quarantine /Applications/OpenArch.app in Terminal, or allow it under System Settings › Privacy & Security.

What language is the interface in?

German, for now. English is next.

Does it run on Intel Macs?

Not yet. Builds are for Apple silicon and macOS 15 or later.

What happens to my Arc data?

OpenArch only reads it. For logins and passwords, macOS asks you once whether OpenArch may use Arc’s key from your keychain.
